# FreeBank app v0.2.6

Your eCash in the app, bidding for FreeBank blocks, and members-only houses.

## Download

From the [releases page](https://github.com/mbdrivechains/freebank-app/releases/latest): the `.deb` for Ubuntu and
Debian, the `.dmg` for a Mac, the `.AppImage` for other Linux. v0.2.4 and later offer this update themselves.

## What's new

- **A simpler layout** (from a walk-through of the whole app): four tabs, **Home · Credit · eCash · Node**, and
  Settings behind the gear. Every function is still there.
  - **Home** is where FreeBank opens: your balance, then **Send**, **Receive** and **From eCash** open right there, as
    on the phone. Receive shows your address straight away.
  - **Credit** holds Notes, Houses, Pools and Bills. What a holder needs comes first; minting, chartering, attesting,
    pools and bills to issue are under **House tools**. Plain words instead of tier, λ, bps, LP and escrow; bills
    mature in days.
  - **eCash** has Wallet and Bidding.
  - **Settings** shows one section at a time: Wallet, Phone, Node & connection, Security, About, and Start over last.
    Security folds to "All clear" when nothing needs doing; long explanations show a sentence, the rest behind More.
- **Several wallets** (with the FreeBank node v0.2.19): Settings › Wallet › Your wallets. Add one from your same
  recovery words (with your passphrase; the words bring it back too), or open a wallet file you already have (it
  keeps its own passphrase, and needs its own backup). The switcher in the header picks the wallet Home, Send, Receive
  and Credit use. Your phone always uses the main wallet, and blocks your node wins pay the main wallet.
- **An eCash tab.** FreeBank makes two wallets of its own in your eCash node (BitWindow's, or one you run), both from
  your recovery words, so the words bring them back:
  - **your eCash wallet**, locked with your wallet passphrase: receive, send, and see its payments. A payment is signed
    with your passphrase and counted by "Approve sends on my phone" like any other;
  - **a bidding wallet**, with no passphrase, holding only what you move into it.

  If your eCash node accepts BitWindow's default login, the tab says so: any program on your computer could then use
  its wallets, the bidding wallet included.
- **Bid for FreeBank blocks** (eCash tab, off unless you turn it on). On each new eCash block, FreeBank bids for the
  next FreeBank block from the bidding wallet, up to a daily cap you set. A winning bid goes to the eCash miner, and
  the block it wins pays your FreeBank wallet. A losing bid costs nothing.
- **Members-only and redeem-only houses** (with the FreeBank node v0.2.19). A house can be chartered so its notes go
  only to its members, or only between the house and the holder. Houses and notes show which; a house's members are
  listed under it, and its owner adds and removes them there; minting can go to a member. Notes of a redeem-only
  house can be gathered onto one of your addresses but not sent on; neither kind can go into a pool.
- **Notes, bills and pools read with the wallet locked** on the FreeBank node v0.2.19: the phone no longer needs "Let
  my phone send" turned on just to show your notes.
- **Sending notes with no address** moves them onto one of your own addresses, which gathers notes spread over several
  addresses (a redeem or a demand needs them together).
- **The phone page:** the iPhone's Home Screen step as three taps, each with the icon Safari shows; members-only
  houses marked.
- **The FreeBank node v0.2.19** names beta "beta" (it said "main"); the header shows "Beta" either way.

## Fixed

- The balance and the header's block now refresh by themselves on every tab. A payment that confirmed used to drop
  its "on its way" line while the balance kept the old figure until you pressed Refresh.
- With nine tabs, the last ones could sit out of sight; the header said "main" on beta (it now names the network).
- The eCash tab's login error pointed to a setting no screen had: Settings › Node & connection now has the eCash node's
  address and login (its password in a file only you can read).
- A phone's held payment the wallet can't pay says so before asking your passphrase, and a failed one says why.
- The Security panel called the peer port public when freebank.conf binds it to this computer.
- Setup's Advanced box: addresses that test green can be saved from there; the sync screen says what the node is doing
  in words, not its log lines.
- Since v0.2.3, Remove FreeBank, Obliterate and Disconnect left old receipts and update notices on screen.

## Security self-audit

- **eCash payments carry eCash's replay protection** (nLockTime 499999999 with non-final inputs, as BitWindow's do),
  so they can't be replayed onto Bitcoin. The eCash node's own wallet doesn't add it.
- **FreeBank signs every eCash payment itself.** The eCash wallets are watch-only in the eCash node: it gets only the
  accounts' public keys, never your passphrase or a private key. A payment from your eCash wallet opens your words with
  your passphrase and signs here; a bid signs with the bidding wallet's key, kept in a file only your account can read.
  Before signing, FreeBank checks what the node prepared: every coin its own, with the transaction it came from (so its
  value can't be misstated), the amount to the address you asked for, change only to its own addresses, the fee as
  shown and its rate no higher than FreeBank asked for, eCash's replay stamp and no timelock; every address the node
  gives is checked against the key, and none far beyond the addresses in use is taken.
  (An independent review found that the first design let any program able to log in to the eCash node, as BitWindow's
  default login allows, spend the main eCash wallet during a payment, and sent your passphrase and keys to whatever
  answered at its address. That design never shipped. A second review of the fixes found further gaps, all closed
  before release: a node lying about coin values across two tries, a payment the node answered with an error escaping
  "Approve sends on my phone", and the others below.)
- **A payment the eCash node doesn't confirm it took** is said to have maybe gone out, stays counted for "Approve sends
  on my phone", and the next payment from that wallet must spend one of its coins, so only one of the two can confirm.
- **Move back** from the bidding wallet shows its fee before anything goes.
- **The eCash keys have a root of their own** (BIP85 XPRV, index 0, from your words; then BIP84 accounts 0 and 1), so they
  are never a Bitcoin wallet's keys for the same words.
- **The eCash node's login:** a node on another computer gets only a login you typed; `https://` addresses are refused
  rather than spoken to over plain http, and an address must be host and port only (so one dressed up to look like this
  computer can't collect its logins); the calls never go through a proxy; on eCash's network the node must have eCash's
  pinned block. The eCash wallet's record and the bidding log are readable only by your account.
- **Bids** are capped by the day's amount you set, counting every bid made in the last 24 hours, whatever the eCash node
  says of it (bids on one coin count once, since only one can confirm); each is recorded before it goes out. A round is
  lost only after three eCash blocks without it, and a lost bid that eCash nodes keep in their mempool is replaced by
  the next bid, which frees its coins, rather than left holding them for two weeks.
- **Minting notes to someone else's address** counts as a payment for "Approve sends on my phone".
- **Members' changes** count as payments for "Approve sends on my phone" (their fee). The guard now reads the fee
  where optional arguments follow it (a house's type, a mint's address); before, those would have made every such call
  ask a phone.
- **apt publishes only after the release is signed:** the workflow that publishes it checks the release key's signature
  and the `.deb` against it first.

## Known limits

- Bidding is the simple version: one bid per eCash block, no raising against rivals.
- A wallet taken off the list stays open until the node restarts (the node can't close one while it runs).

# FreeBank app v0.2.0

The wallet release. Your wallet gets a passphrase and 24 recovery words, Send shows the fee before you pay and tracks
each payment to 3 confirmations, and your phone can reach your desktop wallet from anywhere through
app.ecxfreebank.com.

## What's new

- **Your wallet is protected.** Setting up asks for a passphrase first, then gives you 24 recovery words and asks for
  three of them back. Until then, FreeBank shows no address.
  - Settings > Wallet shows where the wallet is, whether it's locked, the balances and the last backup. It has Back
    up, Restore from a backup file, Restore from your words, Change passphrase, and Show recovery words or xprv
    (behind the passphrase).
  - Payments, credit actions and new keys ask for the passphrase when the wallet is locked, and lock it again after.
  - The words are standard BIP39 words. FreeBank's wallet key comes from them by BIP85 (the WIF application, index
    0), so a BIP85 tool can rebuild it without this app. From v0.3.0 the same words also cover your eCash wallet.
- **Send** is in ECX only, with Max, a speed choice (next block, within an hour, cheapest) and the fee shown before
  you confirm.
  - Each payment gets a receipt on the page: the transaction id with Copy, a link to the explorer, and its
    confirmations counted up to 3.
  - **Speed up** while a payment waits (not for Max payments, which have no change to pay from).
  - **History** lists every transaction, newest first, opens any of them, and exports to CSV.
- **Your phone as a remote.** Settings > Phone > Pair a phone shows a QR code; open it on your phone.
  - Both screens show the same 6-digit code: allow only the phone that shows yours.
  - The phone sees the balance and history, gets an address, and sends up to its daily limit (0.1 ECX unless you
    change it). A payment above the limit waits for you on the desktop and lapses after 10 minutes.
  - With a passphrase on the wallet, turn on "Let my phone send while FreeBank is open": it asks for the passphrase
    once and keeps it in memory only.
  - Everything between phone and desktop is end-to-end encrypted. The relay only passes sealed messages along.
- **Security panel** (Settings > Security): the wallet's passphrase, the node's ports, old unencrypted backups, file
  permissions, the node program's signature, and the eCash side. Red items also show on Home until fixed.
- **Deposit panel** on Home: the address to paste into BitWindow's FreeBank deposit, with a QR code and the steps.
  Receive has a QR code too, and the balance card shows coins that are still arriving.
- **Keep the node running after you close the app** (Settings). The next launch picks it up again.
- **ECX only.** No gram amounts anywhere; gold is off at launch.
- **Mac: results no longer vanish.** macOS showed none of the app's pop-ups, so results and transaction ids were
  lost. They now appear on the page.

## Fixed

From a review of v0.1.1:
- Obliterate could call a funded wallet empty while the node was catching up, or when the coins sat in another wallet
  file. It now gives a balance only when that is certain.
- Wallet backups and `freebank.conf` could be readable by other accounts on the computer. They are now yours only.
- A node program that was never checked against the release signature could still run. It no longer does. A release
  installed by v0.1.0 is downloaded and checked again first.
- Obliterate and the installer now check the node's own file locks, so they never touch a folder another program's
  node is using.
- A backup of one wallet no longer counts for another; folders are labelled by what setup really did; a node folder
  that is a link, and `walletdir=` in `freebank.conf`, are handled.
- The release workflow no longer gives build steps a token that can write to the repository, and a re-run can't
  replace files already published.

Also: Settings (and so Obliterate) can be reached during setup; the download bar no longer shows "0.0 MB" before the
first bytes; Home's recent transactions are the newest ones.

## Upgrading from v0.1.1

- Your wallet has no passphrase yet, so FreeBank asks for one when it opens; "Not now" leaves a banner on Home.
  Protecting it gives you new recovery words.
- The coins you already have stay on the old keys until you press **Move my coins to the new words**. After that one
  set of words covers everything.
- A backup made before the passphrase still spends the coins on the old addresses, with no passphrase. The Security
  panel lists the ones FreeBank made. Delete them, or keep them somewhere safe.

## Security self-audit

**What v0.2.0 protects**
- **Your wallet has a passphrase.** Setting up asks for one first. Payments, credit actions and new keys unlock the
  wallet briefly, then lock it again. Settings > Security shows a red item on Home while the wallet has none.
- **Your recovery words.** The 24 words are kept only in the app's data folder (`wallet/seed.enc`), encrypted with
  your passphrase (Argon2id, then XChaCha20-Poly1305), readable by your account alone. Showing them asks for the
  passphrase.
- **Ports.** The app itself opens no ports; the phone link is an outbound connection. freebankd's RPC answers on this
  computer only (127.0.0.1 and ::1), and ZMQ is off. The peer port, 8455, is public by design and gives no access to
  your wallet. The Security panel reads `freebank.conf`'s `rpcallowip=`, `rpcbind=` and `zmqpub*=` lines, tries the
  RPC port on this computer's network addresses, and turns red if RPC is reachable.
- **Keys and logins on disk.** freebankd writes its login cookie and wallet readable by your account only. So does the
  app, for its phone keys, seed file, send log and backup list. The Security panel warns if another account on the
  computer could read any of them.
- **Plaintext secrets.** The passphrase is never written to disk or logs. "Let my phone send while FreeBank is open"
  keeps it in memory until you turn it off, remove your last phone or quit FreeBank. Wallet backups made before your wallet had a passphrase, such as v0.1.1's "Back
  up wallet", are flagged red with "Show in folder". FreeBank never deletes a backup by itself.
- **The node program.** freebankd is installed only after its `SHA256SUMS.sig` checks out against the FreeBank release
  key built into the app. The app won't start a copy it didn't check.
- **The phone link.** It is end-to-end encrypted (P-256 ECDH, HKDF-SHA256, AES-256-GCM); the relay at
  app.ecxfreebank.com only passes sealed messages along. A pairing code works once, for 5 minutes; both screens show
  a comparison code; and the desktop must allow each phone. A phone can see the balance and history, get an address,
  and send up to its daily limit (0.1 ECX by default). Anything above waits for you on the desktop, and a held send
  lapses after 10 minutes and is never paid after a restart. The relay's own review is `relay/SECURITY_REVIEW.md` in
  the distribution repository.
- **The app's own screens.** The screens' general-purpose node call accepts only a fixed list: their own calls and
  read-only ones. Wallet calls such as `dumpprivkey`, `dumpwallet`, `importwallet`, `backupwallet`, `sethdseed`,
  `walletpassphrase` and `signmessage`, and `stop`, are refused. Links open only for explorer.ecxfreebank.com,
  releases.drivechain.info and FreeBank's GitHub release pages. The page loads no scripts from elsewhere.
- **Your download.** Each release has build attestations and a `SHA256SUMS` signed with the FreeBank release key; "Verify
  your download" in the README shows how to check.

**What v0.2.0 doesn't protect**
- **Malware running as you.** It can read the node's login cookie and use the node as the app does, capture your
  passphrase as you type it, and read your files. The passphrase keeps it from spending only until it has the
  passphrase.
- **Keys from before the passphrase.** Setting a passphrase starts new keys but keeps the old ones, so a backup made
  before still spends coins sent to your old addresses. The Security panel flags such backups; deleting them or
  moving them somewhere safe is up to you.
- **The eCash side.** The eCash node, its wallet and the enforcer belong to BitWindow, or to whoever set them up. The
  app checks their ports from this computer and warns, but can't change them.
- **Other computers.** When the app controls a node on another computer, over Tailscale for example, the port and
  file checks cover this computer only.
- **What the checks can't see.** A port check from this computer shows what a program listens on, not what your
  router or firewall lets through. For a node another program started, the panel reads `freebank.conf` and tries the
  ports, but can't read that program's command-line options.
- **Privacy.** Peers see your IP address. The explorer sees the app's lookups. The relay sees when your phone connects
  and how much it sends, though not what.
- **The app's own commands.** A script that got into the app's page could still ask for the app's own actions, such
  as a send. A locked wallet still asks for your passphrase first.
- **Mac builds** are not notarised. macOS asks you to confirm the first launch, and the signed checksums are your
  check.

## Known limits

- **Intel Macs:** the app is now one universal build for Apple Silicon and Intel Macs, but FreeBank's node has no
  Intel Mac build yet (freebankd v0.2.16 ships Apple Silicon and Linux only). On an Intel Mac, Setup says so. The app
  picks the Intel build up by itself once a FreeBank release carries one.
- One wallet per app. Several wallets are still to be discussed.
- On the Mac, Cmd+Q may close without the "the node keeps running" notice; the node is still left running when that
  setting is on.
- freebankd v0.2.16 can hang if two wallet unlocks land about a second apart (an old Bitcoin Core bug, fixed
  upstream in Core 0.20). The app keeps its own unlocks clear of it; other programs using the same node could still
  trigger it.
